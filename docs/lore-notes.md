## CHARACTERS

### Randy Marsh
**Personality**
- Geology PhD ("Dr. Marsh") who reads as a well-meaning idiot; later seasons pin it on marital misery.
- Early seasons: calm, level-headed dad who gave the boys advice. Later: obsessive fad-chaser who latches onto a cause, goes full man-child, then abandons it when it threatens his reputation.
- Melodramatic ("Staaaaaan!"), hypochondriac, binge drinker (DUI in "Bloody Mary"), habitual coffee-cup carrier, violent in mobs, secretly a genuinely talented musician.
- Caring father underneath; Stan is embarrassed by him constantly.

**Catchphrases / running gags**
- The drawn-out "Staaaaaan!" distress whine.
- Secretly the singer Lorde (pitch-corrected vocals; quit after the hologram-concert fiasco).
- Teenage boy-band past: the Ghetto Avenue Boys, one hit ("You Got It"), fired at 19 for being "too old."
- Nobel Prize for his theory of moderation ("Spontaneous Combustion").
- Accidentally invented warp drive in a pinewood derby → first contact → Earth sealed in a cube force field ("Well, that sucks").
- Microwaved his own testicles to qualify for medical marijuana (now has prosthetics).
- Invented sarcastaball; killed Winnie the Pooh to sell weed in China; WoW character named Falcorn; studied Mongolian in college; dislikes chicken; married Sharon dressed as Christopher Columbus.

**Key relationships**
- Sharon: divorced twice, remarried twice; farm years nearly broke them for good.
- Stan: loves him, embarrasses him. Shelley: oblivious that she brutalizes Stan.
- Grampa Marvin (102, constantly asking to be killed — everyone laughs it off).
- Towelie: business partner at the farm. Stuart: old Boy Scout buddy, bar friend.

**Episode lore nuggets (dialogue seeds)**
- Nobel acceptance, pinewood-derby alien cover-up, the sarcastaball ESPN interview, the Shake Weight marriage fix, getting fired for a drunken Obama-election tirade then quietly rehired, decapitating a "homeless" man with a shotgun.

**Visual note**
- Thick black 70s-style hair with parted bangs + thick black mustache, dimpled chin. Classic: cyan short-sleeve button shirt with two chest pockets (pens in a pocket protector), white undershirt, black pants and shoes. Tegridy era: straw hat, blue plaid shirt under a brown hooded jacket, filthy green-gray pants, muddy brown boots.

### Sharon Marsh
**Personality**
- The sane half of the marriage; foil to Randy's chaos. Loving but stern suburban mom with a fast temper.
- Fiercely protective to criminal extremes; gets swept into town fads (holistic medicine, atheism).
- Wears reading glasses for paperwork (farsighted). Openly resents the Tegridy years; threw a party when Randy was jailed.

**Catchphrases / running gags**
- Buried bodies in her garden to protect Stan ("Spookyfish" — the killer was actually his evil goldfish); held Officer Barbrady captive in the basement.
- Burned down Wall-Mart and Jim's Drugs.
- Scar on her left knee from slipping in a pool (per future-Stan); maiden name Kimble; monthly visits from Aunt Flo.
- The "Shake Weight" marriage rescue; sixth-graders extorting Stan for a photo of her breasts ("your mom has the hottest boobs").

**Key relationships**
- Randy (complicated, thrice-nearly-divorced), Stan (the favored one), Shelley (oblivious to her abuse), Jimbo (her brother), Roy (rebound boyfriend who made Stan chop a year's worth of firewood).

**Episode lore nuggets**
- "Spookyfish" body-burying, "Something Wall-Mart This Way Comes" arson, "Crème Fraiche" cooking-show obsession fallout, "The Pandemic Special" weed-specials fight.

**Visual note**
- Brown wavy shoulder-length hair. Brown sweater with red frilly ruffles at collar and cuffs, dark blue jeans. Formal: brown dress with red trim (or black dress), pearl necklace, gold earrings. Nightgown: cream, knee-length, pink trim, two orange-yellow flowers embroidered on the chest.

### Gerald Broflovski
**Personality**
- The kind, rational dad of the group — moral, stubborn, sarcastic — but corruptible, especially around money.
- Lawyer who genuinely loves suing; city attorney on the City Council. Secret dark streak: Season 20's internet troll Skankhunt42, who posted cruel comments at women and girls for the thrill and accidentally drove Freja Ollegard to suicide.

**Catchphrases / running gags**
- Skankhunt42 troll unmasking; never takes off his yarmulke (even naked — balding underneath).
- Sued the whole town in "Sexual Harassment Panda" (Everyone vs. Everyone); "1TMYMPG" license plate; banned cats then got caught "cheesing" anyway; the hot-tub incident with Randy.

**Key relationships**
- Sheila (met in New Jersey), Kyle (moral instruction), Ike (adopted Canadian son), Stuart (childhood best friend — pizza job together, built the McCormick house as teens, fishing-trip fistfight then reconciliation).

**Episode lore nuggets**
- "Major Boobage" cat-ban hypocrisy, "Chickenpox" fishing trip, "Sexual Harassment Panda" lawsuit cascade, "Two Guys Naked in a Hot Tub" awkwardness.

**Visual note**
- Hot pink yarmulke, khaki jacket over indigo-blue shirt, forest-green trousers. Brown hair with sideburns; full beard trimmed to a point at the chin, mustache disconnected from the beard. Abundant chest hair with two hairless patches.

### Liane Cartman
**Personality**
- Sweet-voiced, soft-spoken, endlessly indulgent; great cook who hands out cookies even when wildly inappropriate.
- Secretly a wildly promiscuous past (crack-whore magazine cover, German scheisse films, half the town at the Drunken Barn Dance). Later seasons: more responsible, stands up to Eric (Super Nanny episode "Tsst", refused the iPad in "HUMANCENTiPAD"). Briefly worked as a realtor ("City People") but quit when Eric feared losing attention.

**Catchphrases / running gags**
- "Big-boned"; "poopsikins" pet names; chocolate chicken pot pie with icing on top.
- Offering male guests a tour of "the rest of the house" that ends in her bedroom.
- Hermaphrodite retcon: first revealed as Eric's father AND mother, later exposed as a town-wide lie to protect the Broncos' season — real father is Jack Tenorman.
- The Nebraska Cartman clan: all fat, all with Eric's speech patterns ("Kickass!", "RESPECT MY AUTHORITAH!"); million-dollar inheritance to Eric.

**Key relationships**
- Eric (spoils rotten; occasional discipline), Mr. Mackey (fling/sex tape), plus half the town's men per the Drunken Barn Dance lore.

**Episode lore nuggets**
- "Cartman's Mom Is a Dirty Slut" paternity saga, "Tsst" (Super Nanny), "Merry Christmas Charlie Manson" (Nebraska trip), "Cat Orgy" (meteor-shower party).

**Visual note**
- Turquoise sweater, brick-red trousers (deliberately mirrors Cartman: sweater = his hat, trousers = his coat). Long brown hair in an old-fashioned bun, rosy lipstick, sweet motherly expression. Formal: black dress with gray lining. Later seasons: light-brown belted trench coat. Bedtime: dark-cyan nightgown or pink robe + green facial mask.

### Stuart McCormick
**Personality**
- Heavy alcoholic, drunk most of the time; brawls with Carol (goes both ways) but tender when sober; never shown harming the kids.
- Surprisingly sweet beats: offered to drink an abortion-pill cocktail for pregnant Carol; suggested naming the new baby Kenny. Amazon Fulfillment Center floor worker who organized a strike for respect. Ex-Cthulhu cultist ("extremely drunk" at the time).

**Catchphrases / running gags**
- The red "SCOTCH" cap; poverty gags (ColecoVision, monochrome TV, dismantled car full of strays); backyard meth lab ("The Poor Kid" → kids in foster care); "I don't want you touching my damn house!" at Randy; Kenny's endless deaths and instant rebirths (Carol counted the 52nd).

**Key relationships**
- Carol (love/hate), Kenny (plays catch; devastated by each death), Kevin (strained, drunken fights), Karen, Gerald (childhood best friend), Randy/Stephen (bar crew).

**Episode lore nuggets**
- "Chickenpox" fishing trip with Gerald, "Cartman Joins NAMBLA" baby plot, "Mysterion Rises" cult reveal, "The Poor Kid" meth-lab arrest, army-helmet photo of Kenny in the movie.

**Visual note**
- Long messy light-brown hair, brown mustache; white shirt under a light-blue jacket with sleeves pushed to the elbow, black belt with gray buckle, blue pants, black shoes; red cap with black border and yellow "SCOTCH" patch; dirt smudges on face and clothes, chain in left pocket. Later: two brown cloth patches sewn onto the coat.

### Carol McCormick
**Personality**
- Heavy drinker who brawls with Stuart; insists he can't hold a job; physically strong (tackled Gerald; gave Stuart a black eye).
- Thick Southern accent (passed to her eldest); devout-ish Catholic family (one of the few that didn't convert to atheism); knitting hobbyist; wants Kenny to become a doctor or lawyer; fully aware of Kenny's death/rebirth cycle and horrified every time anyway.

**Catchphrases / running gags**
- Lime-green "I'm with Stupid" T-shirt (arrow points the wrong way); "I want him out of my house! He ain't worth a shit!"; bought $100 of Ritalin for Kenny's "ADD"; joined the Cthulhu cult for free beer.

**Key relationships**
- Stuart, Kenny (good, aspirational), Kevin, Karen (protective; held her during the Stuart/Kevin brawl).

**Episode lore nuggets**
- "The Poor Kid" arrest, "Best Friends Forever" PSP lecture, "Cartman Joins NAMBLA" pregnancy, "Coon vs. Coon & Friends" cult regret.

**Visual note**
- Lime-green "I'm with Stupid" tee, dark-blue jeans, red hair with Wendy-style bangs, shadowy bruise/filth patch on right cheek and arm. Formal: fake fur coat, red crop top, red miniskirt (later: dark-green blouse, long gray skirt). Nightshirt with bunnies.

### Herbert Garrison
**Personality**
- Narcissistic, sociopathic, dimwitted, profoundly confused; swings between identities and embraces each to vulgar extremes.
- Incompetent teacher (Barnaby Jones tapes, pop-culture "lessons"); casual racist gag streak (annual Christmas "get rid of the Mexicans" motion; "Time to Leave" ghost costumes); breaks the fourth wall; slurs word endings for emphasis; verbally abusive when challenged. Post-presidency: calmer, acts oblivious about the White House years.

**Catchphrases / running gags**
- "YOU GO TO HELL AND YOU DIE!"; "There are no stupid questions, just stupid people"; "Spank my ass and call me Charlie"; "Scissor me timbers!"
- Mr. Hat (possibly sentient puppet = his repressed gay side) → Mr. Twig (murdered by Mr. Hat) → Mr. Slave ("teacher's ass"; Lemmiwinks the gerbil) → Janet (sex change) → lesbian phase → man again.
- Tried to assassinate Kathie Lee Gifford over a childhood talent-show loss (shot Kenny instead).
- Ran for president as a Trump stand-in (Seasons 19–24): "fuck 'em all to death" wall platform, killed the Canadian president, then lost and returned to teaching. Mr. Service, the Secret Service agent turned back into Mr. Hat by Hollywood elites.

**Key relationships**
- Mr. Hat (codependent), Mr. Slave (ex), Allison (lesbian fling), Richard Dawkins (atheist phase), his father (never molested him — his core wound), Cartman (student nemesis).

**Episode lore nuggets**
- "Weight Gain 4000" assassination attempt, "The Death Camp of Tolerance" lawsuit scheme, "Mr. Garrison's Fancy New Vagina" / "Eek, a Penis!", "Where My Country Gone?" campaign launch, "South ParQ Vaccination Special" return.

**Visual note**
- Mostly bald with gray side hair, black-framed glasses, green jacket, dark-green pants, black shoes. As Janet: jacket tailored into a tank top, lipstick, gold earrings, shorter pants, white shoes (bald spot kept). As president: dark-blue suit, red tie, artificial tan, Trump-like blonde hair. Left-handed.

### Mr. Mackey
**Personality**
- One of the few sensible adults; earnest, well-meaning counselor with a terrible temper that grows over the series.
- Level-5 hoarder from childhood trauma (molested by the Woodsy Owl mascot); nervous knuckle-tapping; loves singing; bad with computers; despises kids pooping in urinals (seven-plus euphemisms for crap); secret prankster ("Summer Sucks").

**Catchphrases / running gags**
- "M'kay" verbal tic ("m'bien" in Spanish); "Drugs are bad, m'kay."
- Giant balloon head (tight-tie theory vs. his parents have it too); fired for passing classroom weed around (stolen by Garrison) → became a druggie → married a hippie in India → rescued by the A-Team; "21 years" without sex (disputed); mild psychic "mild-meld"; meteor-shower hot tub.

**Key relationships**
- Ms. Choksondik (lovers; died choking — dark running gag), Liane (fling/sex tape), Principal Victoria (work-spouse energy; he got her fired), Mr. Garrison (self-appointed therapist).

**Episode lore nuggets**
- "Ike's Wee Wee" (drugs, India, A-Team), "Proper Condom Use" (sex ed romance), "Insheeption" (hoarding origin), "Bass to Mouth" (laxative pizza + bus-throwing scheme).

**Visual note**
- Exaggeratedly large balloon head, black thinning hair, big black-rimmed glasses, pointed nose, dimples. Green long-sleeve dress shirt, blue tie, dark-blue slacks, blue leather shoes.

### Jimbo Kern
**Personality**

- Dimwitted despite real technical skill; trigger-happy (shot Kenny thinking he was a platypus); fancies himself Stan's "cool uncle" (hunting trips, swearing, offering alcohol). Sexuality ambiguous: lives with Ned, never married, "everyone is a little gay."

**Catchphrases / running gags**
- "It's comin' right for us!" (legal loophole for shooting animals) → later "we gotta thin the numbers."
- Co-hosts "Huntin' and Killin'" with Ned; snowbound cannibalism (ate Eric Roberts and a film crew); smuggled fireworks from Mexico; guerrilla-tactics bragging; "Kyle, Kyle, he's our man! If he can't win it... I'm out 50 bucks."
- Made Kenny his honorary nephew — then Ned accidentally shot him.

**Key relationships**
- Ned Gerblanski (lifelong partner/best friend, live together), Stan (nephew), Sharon (sister), Randy (brother-in-law, drinking buddy).

**Episode lore nuggets**
- "Volcano" (hunting trip + Scuzzlebutt), "The Mexican Staring Frog" (ratings war), "Cartman's Mom Is Still a Dirty Slut" (cannibalism), "Summer Sucks" (firework smuggling).

**Visual note**
- Orange cap and orange shirt, green vest, brown trousers; nearly bald with wispy light-brown hair under the hat, double chin, broad square jaw; black rectangular eyebrows, no visible nose (Elmer Fudd resemblance); overweight.

**Catchphrases / running gags**

**Key relationships**
- The town's Catholics, Butters (friend), the Pope (adversary), Kenny (officiated many funerals — except the semi-permanent one, where Garrison filled in).

## LOCATIONS

### South Park Church [SECONDARY — page blocked]
- Roman Catholic parish run by Father Maxi; white wooden church with a steeple.
- Site of hellfire sermons, confessional gags (Maxi caught with Mrs. Donovan; Cartman strangled), Kenny funerals, weddings and funerals of the week.
- Lore beats: Maxi's Vatican crusade to rewrite doctrine ("Red Hot Catholic Love"); the Stations-of-the-Cross trick on Kyle; the "Kumboni" cleanup-crew incident.

### City Hall
- White-stone civic building: oval top, square pillared base, doorway between two pillars; Mayor McDaniels' office directly above the door (gray walls, brown floor, town-logo rug, wooden desk flanked by her two aides).
- She lives there (pink bedroom seen in "Merry Christmas Charlie Manson!"). Council chamber seats 9 members; a smaller meeting room hosts the crisis talks.
- Lore beats: Christmas protests ("Mr. Hankey"), the flag debate ("Chef Goes Nanners"), Randy's trial ("Season Finale"), the annual tree-lighting ("Red Sleigh Down"), Cartman's mayoral reign ("The Wacky Molestation Adventure"), celebrity-crisis summits ("200/201"), the Wall-Mart destruction fallout. Quest texture: town meetings that solve nothing; aide-flanked mayor giving non-answers.

### Tegridy Farms [SECONDARY — page blocked]
- Randy's hemp farm bought in Season 22; dragged the whole family out of town; "Tegridy" weed brand.
- Family miserable there the entire run (Sharon's open resentment; Shelley's "marijuana problem"; Stan's embarrassment).
- Towelie as business partner; brief "Techridy" AI rebrand; sold at the end of "Sickofancy" (Season 27). In the Post Covid timeline, Stan burned it down.
- Texture: fields, drying barn, roadside stand; Randy's straw-hat era.

### Tweek Bros. Coffeehouse [SECONDARY — page blocked]
- Run by Richard and Mrs. Tweak on Main Street; every cup is secretly laced with meth (they blame Tweek's issues on ADD).
- Richard's stories never go anywhere ("a light rain in the middle of a dusty afternoon..."). The Goth kids pointedly drink coffee elsewhere (Benny's / Village Inn).
- Texture: jittery family shop, coffee IV humor, "find your center" calm-down corner.

### Jimbo's Guns
- Brown storefront with a wagon wheel out front; glass handgun counter, rifles on the wall, orange walls and gray floor, a mirror for posing with new purchases; later dark-green walls with "Stand Your Ground" signage.
- Lore beats: Garrison bought the Kathie Lee Gifford assassination rifle here ("Weight Gain 4000"); Stan's evil clone threw Garrison through the window ("An Elephant Makes Love to a Pig"); Cartman bought a .22 here ("World War Zimmerman" — Stand Your Ground explained); the alley beside it hid Muhammad ("201"); game quest hub (gas mask, Scuzzlebutt paw taxidermy).

### City Wok
- Chinese restaurant run by Tuong Lu Kim; his accent makes it sound like "shitty wok." Every menu item starts with "City" (City Beef, City Chicken, City Shrimp).
- Doubles as City Airlines: one yellow Cessna, tail 432G, which crashed. Basement hosts "Cock Magic" — roosters playing Magic: The Gathering, $5 admission.
- Lore beats: turf war with City Sushi; robbed by Timmy & Jimmy's Crips crew ("Krazy Kripples"); break-dancing Lu Kim ("Pajama Day").
- Texture: traditional Chinese decor, counter partition plus table seating.

### South Park Elementary [SECONDARY — page blocked]
- Brick school building, flagpole, playground; the 4th-grade classroom (Garrison's), Mackey's office, the cafeteria (Chef's domain), the gym.
- Lore beats: the gender war ("Eek, a Penis!"), Wieners Out, talent shows, the Chef Hall of Honor memorial, Cartman's "How to Kill a Midget" presentation.
- Texture: the hallway/cafeteria hub that replaced the Bus Stop as the social center after Season 9.

### Stark's Pond [SECONDARY — page blocked]
- The town's winter ice-skating pond; Wendy's figure-skating showcase in the movie (figure-eight, salchow, triple lutz).
- Texture: frozen pond, hockey pickup games, school skating trips; seasonal winter-only access.

### The Bus Stop
- The iconic sign; the boys' lineup — Stan + Kyle together, Kenny far right, Cartman by the sign; the hangout where episodes opened with news and schemes.
- Faded after Season 9 as the cafeteria/hallways took over; last group sighting in "#REHASH"; Streaming Wars: only Kyle & Kenny still queue.
- Lore beats: gender-swapped with the girls in "Moss Piglets"; parallel-universe twin in "Joining the Panderverse"; series' most merchandised image.
- Texture: snowy sign, bench, quiet before-school banter — nostalgia trigger for any daily-login scene.

### Skeeter's Bar (now Skeeter's Wine Bar)
- The dads' bar: regulars Randy, Gerald, Jimbo, Ned, Stuart, Skeeter. Plain interior upgraded to booths and a jukebox; briefly renamed "Wine Bar" in "Tweek x Craig."
- Lore beats: Randy's Wheel of Fortune viewing party ("With Apologies to Jesse Jackson"); Gerald learned of Freja's suicide here; Garrison sang "Where Has My Country Gone?" here; memberberries stashed behind the back door (game quest); rats in the cellar.
- Texture: redneck bar, pool table, darts, Wheel of Fortune on the TV.

### Raisins
- Hooters-style breastaurant; famous for chicken wings and flirty underage waitresses all named after cars (Lexus, Porsche).
- Lore beats: Butters fell in love with Lexus ("Raisins"); her flirting-for-tips sparked his "beautiful sadness" speech; Stan's post-Wendy goth phase began after a visit; bouncer Maury; parents appalled by the concept.
- Texture: neon, wings baskets, tip-baiting smiles; bouncer at the door.

### South Park Mall
- Cluster of businesses; Fingerbang performed here for the mall boss; Kenny died by elevator here (Randy replaced him mid-set); Randy's Black Friday trilogy as a security guard (shoppers murdering each other over 96%-off deals; George R.R. Martin stalling the opening).
- Stupid Spoiled Whore store (Paris Hilton); Hot Topic sparked the vampire fad — burned down by the Goths ("The Ungroundable"); abandoned after the Amazon Fulfillment Center opened ("Unfulfilled"); alien ship crashed into it (Stick of Truth; government claimed a new Taco Bell was coming); Cartman's PSP line ("Best Friends Forever").
- Texture: food court, fountain, dying retail — Black Friday chaos mode.

### Main Street
- The town's commercial spine; storefronts shift episode to episode: Bijou Cinema, Tom's Rhinoplasty, Bank of South Park, Freeman's Tacos, Photo Dojo, Tele's (T.V. World), Les Bos, Luau's Toys, Tweek Bros. Coffeehouse, City Wok.
- Lore beats: Cow Days Running of the Bulls; the Chef Aid concert; the film festival ("Chef's Chocolate Salty Balls"); Garrison's march; the "Pinkeye" zombie attack; Stan's banishment ("Douche and Turd"); Mel Gibson's fan march.
- Early seasons used it as the whole-town establishing shot with the town sign. Texture: parades, riots, protests, mobs — the town's stage.

### The Town of South Park
- Tiny mountain town in Colorado, founded 1867 by 14 founders from the East Coast (including Jimbo's grandfather).
- Mostly Roman Catholic, fiercely fad-prone; landmarks: Stark's Pond, church, City Hall, school, mall, Tegridy Farms outskirts.
- Texture beats: town meetings that solve nothing; angry mobs; celebrity invasions; the mayor's non-answers; "they took our jobs" rednecks; annual fads (metrosexual, vampire, memberberries). The show's running joke that nothing ever really changes — and nobody notices.
